# Lesson 35: The *-v* Participle, Reported Speech, *-ja* and *-mine*

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 260 · § 318–333 · [← Previous](lesson-34.md) · [Next →](lesson-36.md)

## Key ideas

- The *-v* participle describes a noun, like English "-ing" before a noun.
- The oblique mood in *-vat* marks something you know only from others.
- *-ja* makes a word for the doer of an action.
- *-mine* makes a noun for the action itself.
- All these forms are built on the *-ma* infinitive.

## The present participle (§ 318–320)

Take the stem of the *-ma* infinitive and add *-v*.

| Infinitive | Participle | Example |
|---|---|---|
| kõndima | kõndiv | *kõndiv mees* "a walking man" |
| naeratama | naeratav | *naeratav laps* "a smiling child" |
| sõitma | sõitev | *sõitev auto* "a moving car" |
| tõusma | tõusev | *tõusev päike* "the rising sun" |

It declines like an adjective, and the genitive always ends in *-a*.

| Stem type | Nom. | Gen. | Part. | Ill. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vowel stem | naeratav | naeratava | naeratavat | naeratavasse |
| Consonant stem | sõitev | sõitva | sõitvat | sõitvasse |

Consonant stems have an *e* only in the nominative singular. After a short vowel, the final consonant doubles there: *võtma* → *võttev* but plural *võtvad*; *jätma* → *jättev* but plural *jätvad*.

## The passive participle in *-dav/-tav* (§ 321)

This form often matches English "-able":

| Participle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| joodav | drinkable |
| kuuldav | audible |
| kantav | wearable |

## Participles instead of clauses (§ 322–323)

After verbs of perceiving, thinking or feeling, an *et* clause can be shortened. The old subject becomes an object, and the verb becomes *-vat*.

| With a clause | Shortened | English |
|---|---|---|
| *Ma näen, et rong tuleb.* | *Ma näen rongi tulevat.* | I see the train coming. |
| *Ma kuulen, et lapsed naeravad.* | *Ma kuulen lapsi naervat.* | I hear the children laughing. |
| *Ma loodan, et õde võidab.* | *Ma loodan õe võitvat.* | I hope my sister wins. |

- A singular total object may be genitive, as with *õe*.
- If both clauses share a subject, use a reflexive: *Ma tunnen end väsinud olevat.* "I feel tired."
- After impersonal *tundub* or *paistab*, the old subject remains a subject: *Tundub, et sa oled väsinud.* becomes *Sa tundud väsinud olevat.*

## The oblique mood (§ 324–326)

Use *-vat* when reporting something you have heard. The form is the same for every person, and the negative just adds *ei*.

| Tense | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Present | *Ta sõitvat homme Soome.* | *Ta ei sõitvat homme Soome.* |
| Past | *Ta olevat lahkunud.* | *Ta ei olevat lahkunud.* |

A reporting clause can be stated or just implied: *Hinnad tõusvat.* "Prices are said to be rising."

In storytelling, the *-nud* form alone can act as a reported past: *Rebane leidnud juustu.* "A fox, they say, found some cheese."

## Doers: *-ja* (§ 327–330)

Add *-ja* to the stem of the *-ma* infinitive.

| Verb | Noun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ehitama | ehitaja | builder |
| aitama | aitaja | helper |
| tõlkima | tõlkija | translator |
| sõitma | sõitja | driver, rider |

- Its forms are *-ja*, *-ja*, *-jat*, *-jaid*.
- A stem ending in *-e* changes it to *i*: *minema* → *minija*, *tulema* → *tulija*, *pesema* → *pesija*.
- It can stand before another noun: *võitja meeskond* "the winning team".

An *-ja* word names someone by what they do. A *-v* participle describes what is going on: *mängija* is a player, while *mängiv laps* is a child who is playing.

## Action nouns: *-mine* (§ 331–332)

Add *-mine* to the same stem.

| Verb | Noun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ujuma | ujumine | *Ujumine on tervislik.* "Swimming is healthy." |
| koristama | koristamine | *Koristamine võtab aega.* "Cleaning takes time." |
| kohtuma | kohtumine | *Homme on kohtumine.* "There's a meeting tomorrow." |

Its forms are *-mine*, *-mise*, *-mist*, *-misi*.

## Exercises

**A. Give the nominative and genitive of the *-v* participle.**

1. kõndima
2. tõusma
3. kandma
4. naeratama

**B. Change to the oblique mood, as if you only heard it.**

1. Ta elab Narvas.
2. Nad ei tule.
3. Ta sõitis ära.
4. Hinnad on kõrged.

**C. Form the word requested and translate the sentence.**

1. *õpetama*, doer: Mu õde on ___.
2. *ujuma*, action noun: ___ on tore.
3. Shorten: *Ma näen, et buss tuleb.*
4. Translate: "They say the shop is closed."
