# Lesson 25: The Translative and Emphatic -ki/-gi

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 159 · § 174–185 · [← Previous](lesson-24.md) · [Next →](lesson-26.md)

## Key ideas

- The translative ends in *-ks*, added to the genitive. It answers *kelleks?* and *milleks?*
- Its core idea is change: what someone or something becomes.
- It also marks purpose, time limits, rank and many set verb phrases.
- *-ki/-gi* adds emphasis, often like "even" or "(not) at all".

## Forming the translative (§ 174)

| Nominative | Genitive | Translative | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| arst | arsti | arstiks | doctor |
| laulja | laulja | lauljaks | singer |
| tark | targa | targaks | wise |
| soe | sooja | soojaks | warm |
| haige | haige | haigeks | sick |

*Ta tahab saada arstiks.* "She wants to become a doctor." *Ilm läheb soojaks.* "The weather is getting warm."

## What it is used for (§ 175–178)

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Making or judging | *See muusika teeb mind rõõmsaks.* | This music makes me cheerful. |
| | *Pean seda heaks mõtteks.* | I consider that a good idea. |
| Serving as something | *Ta on mulle toeks.* | She is a support to me. |
| Purpose | *Mida sa tahad hommikusöögiks?* | What do you want for breakfast? |
| | *Ostan selle sünnipäevaks.* | I'm buying this for a birthday. |
| Length of a planned stay | *Sõidan kolmeks päevaks Riiga.* | I'm going to Riga for three days. |
| Deadline | *Tee see reedeks valmis.* | Have it ready by Friday. |
| Rank or order | *Ta tuli võistlusel kolmandaks.* | She came third in the competition. |
| | *Esiteks on mul kiire, teiseks olen väsinud.* | First, I'm busy; second, I'm tired. |

## Set verb phrases (§ 179)

The translative often joins a verb to name the result of an action.

| Phrase | English |
|---|---|
| selgeks õppima | to learn thoroughly |
| pooleks lõikama | to cut in half |
| kuivaks pühkima | to wipe dry |
| punaseks värvima | to paint red |

## Agreement and pronouns (§ 180–182)

An adjective with a translative noun also takes *-ks*: *Ta sai heaks arstiks.* "He became a good doctor."

A translative adjective used alone in the predicate stays singular, even with a plural subject. Compare the nominative predicate:

| Translative | Nominative |
|---|---|
| *Õhtud lähevad külmaks.* "The evenings are getting cold." | *Õhtud on külmad.* "The evenings are cold." |

Personal pronouns use the long genitive only: *minuks, sinuks, temaks, meieks, teieks, nendeks*.

## Emphatic -ki/-gi (§ 183–185)

The last sound of the word chooses the form.

| Last sound | Particle | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *b, d, f, g, h, k, p, s, š, t* | -ki | leibki, vendki, kasski, raamatki |
| *l, m, n, r, v, z, ž*, any vowel | -gi | laulgi, koergi, tubagi |

It attaches to nearly any word. Translation depends on context:

| Example | English |
|---|---|
| *Lapsedki said aru.* | Even the children understood. |
| *Ma ei teadnudki.* | I had no idea. |
| *Ta ei joonud tilkagi.* | She didn't drink a single drop. |
| *See polegi nii kallis.* | It isn't so expensive after all. |
| *Nii see ongi.* | That's just how it is. |

Some words contain it permanently, such as *keegi, miski, kunagi, isegi, ükski, siiski, iialgi, kumbki*. When such a word is declined, the particle stays at the very end:

| Base | Case form |
|---|---|
| keegi | kellelegi, kellegagi |
| miski | millestki |

*Ma ei rääkinud kellelegi.* "I didn't tell anyone." *Ta pole kunagi Riias käinud.* "She has never been to Riga."

## Useful phrases

| Estonian | English |
|---|---|
| Saa terveks! | Get well! |
| Jää terveks! | Take care! |
| näiteks | for example |
| Ma ei teagi. | I don't really know. |
| Kelleks sa tahad saada? | What do you want to be? |
