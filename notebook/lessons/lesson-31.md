# Lesson 31: The Partitive Plural

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 213 · § 252–266 · [← Previous](lesson-30.md) · [Next →](lesson-32.md)

## Key ideas

- The partitive plural does the same jobs for plural nouns that the partitive singular does for singular nouns.
- It ends in *-id*, *-sid*, or a single vowel (*-e*, *-i*, *-u*).
- Numbers from 2 up still take the partitive **singular**. *Palju* and *vähe* take the partitive plural with countable nouns.
- From now on, treat the partitive plural as a fourth form to learn with every word.

## When to use it (§ 252–255)

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Partial or unfinished object | *Ma otsin võtmeid.* | I'm looking for keys. |
| Unspecified amount as subject | *Õues on koeri.* | There are (some) dogs outside. |
| After a container or amount | *kast õunu* | a box of apples |
| After *palju* and *vähe* | *palju kohvikuid*, *vähe busse* | many cafés, few buses |
| After some prepositions | *enne jõule* | before Christmas |
| Headings meaning "some ..." | *Lugusid merest* | Stories from the Sea |
| "One of the ..." | *See on parimaid filme, mida olen näinud.* | This is one of the best films I have seen. |

Compare the two counting patterns:

| Quantity word | Case | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *palju*, *vähe* | partitive plural | *palju busse* |
| number 2 or higher | partitive singular | *kaks bussi* |

## Three formation groups (§ 256–263)

### Group 1: *-id* (§ 256)

Most longer words with a partitive singular in *-t* add *-id* to the genitive stem. One-syllable words with a long vowel also belong here, and their vowel shortens or changes.

| Nominative | Genitive | Part. sg. | Part. pl. | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *töö* | *töö* | *tööd* | **töid** | work |
| *tee* | *tee* | *teed* | **teid** | road |
| *kohvik* | *kohviku* | *kohvikut* | **kohvikuid** | café |
| *mantel* | *mantli* | *mantlit* | **mantleid** | coat |
| *võõras* | *võõra* | *võõrast* | **võõraid** | strange, foreign |
| *kollane* | *kollase* | *kollast* | **kollaseid** | yellow |

### Group 2: *-sid* (§ 257)

This group is mainly two-syllable words with a short first syllable, plus feminine words in *-nna*. Add *-sid* to the genitive form.

| Nominative | Genitive | Part. sg. | Part. pl. | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *nina* | *nina* | *nina* | **ninasid** | nose |
| *tädi* | *tädi* | *tädi* | **tädisid** | aunt |
| *suvi* | *suve* | *suve* | **suvesid** | summer |
| *meri* | *mere* | *merd* | **meresid** | sea |
| *sõbranna* | *sõbranna* | *sõbrannat* | **sõbrannasid** | female friend |

### Group 3: a final vowel (§ 258–262)

Look at the vowel at the end of the partitive singular, then swap it.

| Part. sg. ends in | Part. pl. ends in | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-i* or *-u* | **-e** | *kappi* → **kappe**, *tundi* → **tunde**, *turgu* → **turge** |
| *-e* | **-i** | *tamme* → **tammi**, *kaske* → **kaski** |
| *-a*, after *e o u ä ö ü* | **-i** | *kella* → **kelli**, *kohta* → **kohti**, *kurba* → **kurbi** |
| *-a*, after *a i õ*, *ei*, *äi* | **-u** | *panka* → **panku**, *linna* → **linnu**, *õuna* → **õunu**, *leiba* → **leibu** |

Other diphthongs follow their first vowel, so *laeva* gives **laevu**.

If the genitive ends in *-e* but the partitive singular ends in a consonant, use *-i* (§ 260):

| Nominative | Genitive | Part. sg. | Part. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| *uus* | *uue* | *uut* | **uusi** |
| *noor* | *noore* | *noort* | **noori** |
| *uks* | *ukse* | *ust* | **uksi** |
| *naine* | *naise* | *naist* | **naisi** |
| *tavaline* | *tavalise* | *tavalist* | **tavalisi** |
| *vesi* | *vee* | *vett* | **vesi** |

### Exceptions (§ 263)

| Pattern | Words |
|---|---|
| *-e*, because the stem ends in *j* | *tühi → tühje, kuri → kurje* |
| *-e*, with short *u* before final *-a* | *tuba → tube, luba → lube, nuga → nuge, muna → mune* |
| *-u* instead of *-i* | *neli → nelju, väli → välju* |
| *-i* instead of *-u* | *pikk → pikki, silm → silmi, king → kingi* |

## Two possible forms (§ 264–265)

Some words accept both a vowel form and a *-sid* form:

| Word | Vowel form | *-sid* form |
|---|---|---|
| *kala* "fish" | *kalu* | *kalasid* |
| *sõna* "word" | *sõnu* | *sõnasid* |
| *rida* "row" | *ridu* | *ridasid* |
| *lipp* "flag" | *lippe* | *lippusid* |

## All the cases at a glance (§ 266)

| Case | Estonian name | Question | *õpik* sg. | *õpik* pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | *nimetav* | *kes? mis?* | *õpik* | *õpikud* |
| Genitive | *omastav* | *kelle? mille?* | *õpiku* | *õpikute* |
| Partitive | *osastav* | *keda? mida?* | *õpikut* | *õpikuid* |
| Illative | *sisseütlev* | *kellesse? millesse?* | *õpikusse* | *õpikutesse* |
| Inessive | *seesütlev* | *kelles? milles?* | *õpikus* | *õpikutes* |
| Elative | *seestütlev* | *kellest? millest?* | *õpikust* | *õpikutest* |
| Allative | *alaleütlev* | *kellele? millele?* | *õpikule* | *õpikutele* |
| Adessive | *alalütlev* | *kellel? millel?* | *õpikul* | *õpikutel* |
| Ablative | *alaltütlev* | *kellelt? millelt?* | *õpikult* | *õpikutelt* |
| Translative | *saav* | *kelleks? milleks?* | *õpikuks* | *õpikuteks* |
| Terminative | *rajav* | *kelleni? milleni?* | *õpikuni* | *õpikuteni* |
| Essive | *olev* | *kellena? millena?* | *õpikuna* | *õpikutena* |
| Comitative | *kaasaütlev* | *kellega? millega?* | *õpikuga* | *õpikutega* |
| Abessive | *ilmaütlev* | *kelleta? milleta?* | *õpikuta* | *õpikuteta* |

A partitive singular can look exactly like a short illative, so use the sentence to tell them apart: *Ma näen seda kooli* (partitive) and *Ma lähen kooli* (short illative).

## Exercises

**A. Give the partitive plural.**

1. *laud (laua, lauda)*
2. *pood (poe, poodi)*
3. *kuusk (kuuse, kuuske)*
4. *vana (vana, vana)*
5. *hobune (hobuse, hobust)*
6. *sõnaraamat (sõnaraamatu, sõnaraamatut)*
7. *kurk (kurgi, kurki)*
8. *must (musta, musta)*

**B. Put the word in brackets into the correct form.**

1. *Poes on palju ___ (klient).*
2. *Mul on kaks ___ (õde).*
3. *Pargis on vähe ___ (pink).*
4. *Ostsin kolm ___ (pirn).*
5. *Aias kasvab palju ___ (roos).*
6. *Ta on meie linna ___ (tuntum arst).* ("one of the best-known doctors")

**C. Translate into Estonian.**

1. We have many friends.
2. I am buying flowers.
3. There are (some) cars in the yard.
4. This is one of the best restaurants in town.
