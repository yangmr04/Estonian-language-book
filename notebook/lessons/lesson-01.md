# Lesson 1: Pronouns and the Present Tense

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 16 · § 1–7 · [Next →](lesson-02.md)

## Key ideas

- Each personal pronoun has a long form and a short form.
- Estonian has no grammatical gender.
- A present-tense verb is a stem plus a personal ending.
- The present tense also covers the future.

## Personal pronouns (§ 1–2)

| Person | Long form | Short form | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | *mina* | *ma* | I |
| 2nd sg. | *sina* | *sa* | you (one person) |
| 3rd sg. | *tema* | *ta* | he, she |
| 1st pl. | *meie* | *me* | we |
| 2nd pl. | *teie* | *te* | you (several people) |
| 3rd pl. | *nemad* | *nad* | they |

- The short form is the everyday default.
- Use the long form for emphasis or contrast: *Mina jään, aga sina lähed.* "**I** am staying, but **you** are going."
- *Tema/ta* means both "he" and "she". Context tells you which one is meant.

## Present-tense endings (§ 3–4)

Verbs change for person and time. Remove the *-n* from the "I" form to find the present stem, then add the ending.

| Person | Ending | *elama* "to live" | *ootama* "to wait" |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ma* | *-n* | *elan* | *ootan* |
| *sa* | *-d* | *elad* | *ootad* |
| *ta* | *-b* | *elab* | *ootab* |
| *me* | *-me* | *elame* | *ootame* |
| *te* | *-te* | *elate* | *ootate* |
| *nad* | *-vad* | *elavad* | *ootavad* |

The ending shows the person, so the pronoun can be dropped when the meaning is clear: *Elan Tartus.* "I live in Tartu."

## The verb "to be" (§ 5)

| Person | *olema* |
|---|---|
| *ma* | *olen* |
| *sa* | *oled* |
| *ta* | **on** |
| *me* | *oleme* |
| *te* | *olete* |
| *nad* | **on** |

The two third-person forms are the same, and neither takes the usual *-b* or *-vad*.

## Talking about the future (§ 6)

There is no separate future tense. Use the present, and add a time word when needed: *Homme ma ootan.* "Tomorrow I will wait."

## Polite "you" (§ 7)

*Teie/te* is also the polite way to address one person, as with French *vous*. The verb stays in the plural form: *Kas te olete õpetaja?* "Are you a teacher?"

## Useful phrases

| Estonian | English |
|---|---|
| *Tere!* | Hello! |
| *Tere hommikust!* | Good morning! |
| *Head aega!* | Goodbye! |
| *Aitäh! / Tänan!* | Thank you! |
