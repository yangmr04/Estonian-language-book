# Lesson 22: More on Infinitives, -ma Cases and the Gerund

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 135 · § 144–153 · [← Previous](lesson-21.md) · [Next →](lesson-23.md)

## Key ideas

- In a chain of verbs, each infinitive is chosen by the verb that governs it.
- An adverbial particle comes before an infinitive but after a conjugated verb.
- The *-ma* form can take case endings, like a noun.
- The gerund in *-des* shows an action happening at the same time as another.

## Chains of infinitives (§ 144)

Work link by link. Each verb decides only the form of the next one.

| Sentence | Links | English |
|---|---|---|
| *Tahan õppida ujuma.* | tahan → *-da*, õppida → *-ma* | I want to learn to swim. |
| *Me peame proovima aidata.* | peame → *-ma*, proovima → *-da* | We must try to help. |

Word order can shift without changing these links: *Me peame aidata proovima.*

## Particles with infinitives (§ 145)

| *-ma* | *-da* | Present |
|---|---|---|
| *ära minema* | *ära minna* | *lähen ära* |
| *üles tõusma* | *üles tõusta* | *tõusen üles* |
| *kaasa võtma* | *kaasa võtta* | *võtan kaasa* |
| *tagasi andma* | *tagasi anda* | *annan tagasi* |

*Lähen kohe ära. Pean kohe ära minema. Tahan kohe ära minna.* "I'm leaving at once. I must leave at once. I want to leave at once."

## Case forms of the -ma infinitive (§ 146–151)

The *-ma* form began as a kind of verbal noun, which explains why it can be declined.

| Form | Case | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ujuma* | short illative | going off to do it | *Lähme ujuma!* "Let's go swimming!" |
| *ujumas* | inessive | in the middle of doing it | *Lapsed on ujumas.* "The children are out swimming." |
| *ujumast* | elative | coming from doing it | *Tuleme ujumast.* "We're coming back from swimming." |
| *ujumata* | abessive | without doing it | *Ta lahkus ujumata.* "She left without swimming." |
| *ujumaks* | translative | for the purpose of doing it | *Tulin ujumaks.* "I came in order to swim." |

**-mas** also shows that something is about to happen: *Rong on saabumas.* "The train is arriving." *Käima* + *-mas* means going somewhere regularly and coming back: *Talvel käime suusatamas.* "In winter we go skiing." *Olemas* means "existing, available": *Kas võti on olemas?* "Do you have the key?"

**-mast** follows some verbs and expressions: *Olen tüdinud ootamast.* "I'm tired of waiting." It also appears in thanks: *Aitäh helistamast!* "Thanks for calling!"

**-mata** can follow *ilma*, and it can describe a noun like an English "un-" participle: *ilma kedagi tervitamata* "without greeting anyone", *pesemata nõud* "unwashed dishes", *Kiri on lõpetamata.* "The letter is unfinished."

**-maks** is mainly a written shortcut for an *et* clause. *Ta kolis linna leidmaks tööd.* means the same as *Ta kolis linna, et tööd leida.* "He moved to town to find work."

## The gerund (§ 152–153)

Replace the *-da* infinitive's ending as follows. The forms do not change for person.

| Infinitive ending | Gerund ending | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-da* | *-des* | *ujuda → ujudes, naerda → naerdes* |
| *-ta* | *-tes* | *pesta → pestes, vaadata → vaadates* |
| *-a* | *-es* | *panna → pannes, käia → käies* |

*Ta tuli naerdes tuppa.* "She came into the room laughing." *Koju minnes ostan piima.* "On the way home I'll buy milk."

The gerund comes from an old inessive of the *-da* infinitive. No other case of that infinitive survives.
