# Lesson 7: The Genitive Singular

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 41 · § 31–41 · [← Previous](lesson-06.md) · [Next →](lesson-08.md)

## Key ideas

- Estonian has 14 cases, but they are built very regularly.
- The genitive shows possession and always ends in a vowel.
- The final vowel is not predictable from the nominative, so learn the pair.
- Most other singular cases are the genitive plus a fixed ending.

## Why the case system is manageable (§ 31)

Nouns, adjectives, pronouns and numbers all have 14 cases, singular and plural. A handful of base forms do most of the work, and the endings are stable and join on without change.

## What the genitive does (§ 32–34)

It answers *kelle?* "whose?" for people and *mille?* "of what?" for things. The owner comes first.

| Question | Answer | English |
|---|---|---|
| *Kelle kass?* | *Arsti kass.* | The doctor's cat. |
| *Mille aken?* | *Bussi aken.* | The bus window. |

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| In place of English "of" | *linna keskus* | the centre of the town |
|  | *päeva lõpp* | the end of the day |
| A chain of genitives | *arsti koera nimi* | the name of the doctor's dog |
| Adjective + genitive noun: the adjective is genitive too | *punase auto juht* | the driver of the red car |

## Learn two forms (§ 35–37)

A vocabulary entry gives both nominative and genitive, because the genitive is the base for all other singular cases except the partitive, and for the nominative plural.

| Ending | Meaning | From *buss*, gen. *bussi* | From *pudel*, gen. *pudeli* |
|---|---|---|---|
| *-s* | in | *bussis* | *pudelis* |
| *-ga* | with | *bussiga* | *pudeliga* |
| *-ta* | without | *bussita* | *pudelita* |
| *-d* | nominative plural | *bussid* | *pudelid* |

These endings always attach to a word and never stand alone.

## Words ending in a consonant (§ 38)

The genitive adds one of four vowels.

| Added vowel | Examples |
|---|---|
| *-a* | *õun, õuna* "apple"; *koer, koera* "dog"; *mets, metsa* "forest" |
| *-e* | *järv, järve* "lake"; *lill, lille* "flower"; *keel, keele* "language" |
| *-i* | *kass, kassi* "cat"; *arst, arsti* "doctor"; *paber, paberi* "paper" |
| *-u* | *hommik, hommiku* "morning"; *sõnastik, sõnastiku* "dictionary" |

## Foreign names (§ 39)

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| Consonant-final names usually take -i | *Pariis, Pariisi*; *Brown, Browni* |
| Some names in -s take -e | *Texas, Texase*; *Las Vegas, Las Vegase* |

## Words ending in a vowel (§ 40)

Usually the genitive looks just like the nominative: *auto*, *kino*, *õhtu* "evening", *kala* "fish", *müüja* "salesperson", *Pärnu*.

A word's context tells you the case: *Müüja ootab.* "The salesperson is waiting." versus *müüja auto* "the salesperson's car".

Not all of them stay the same. Some change the final vowel, as in *lumi, lume* "snow" and *tuli, tule* "fire". Others change in the stem (see Lesson 8).

## Words in -ne (§ 41)

Most of these swap *-ne* for *-se*.

| Nominative | Genitive | English |
|---|---|---|
| *punane* | *punase* | red |
| *sinine* | *sinise* | blue |
| *õpilane* | *õpilase* | pupil |
| *soomlane* | *soomlase* | Finn |
| *hobune* | *hobuse* | horse |

A few two-syllable words in *-ne* stay unchanged: *aine* "substance", *kaine* "sober".

## Useful phrases

| Estonian | English |
|---|---|
| *Kelle oma see on?* | Whose is this? |
| *See on minu oma.* | It's mine. |
| *Minu sõbra nimi on Mari.* | My friend's name is Mari. |
| *Kus on bussipeatus?* | Where is the bus stop? |
| *Mis kell on?* | What time is it? |
| *Eesti keel on ilus.* | Estonian is beautiful. |
