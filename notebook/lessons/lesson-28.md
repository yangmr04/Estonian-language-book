# Lesson 28: Total and Partial Objects

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 185 · § 215–227 · [← Previous](lesson-27.md) · [Next →](lesson-29.md)

## Key ideas

- A direct object can be nominative, genitive or partitive.
- A **total object** needs an affirmative sentence, a completed action and a whole object.
- If any of those conditions fails, the object is **partial**, which means partitive.
- A total object with a present verb points to the future.

## Three possible cases (§ 215–217)

Total objects depend on the sentence type and on number.

| | Singular object | Plural object |
|---|---|---|
| Command | nominative: *Osta pilet!* | nominative: *Osta piletid!* |
| Statement | genitive: *Ostsin pileti.* | nominative: *Ostsin piletid.* |

## The partial object (§ 218–220)

The partitive is required if **any** of these applies:

| Condition | Total | Partial |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | *Ostsin pileti.* "I bought the ticket." | *Ma ei ostnud piletit.* "I didn't buy the ticket." |
| Ongoing, repeated or unfinished action | *Kirjutasin artikli valmis.* "I finished writing the article." | *Kirjutasin artiklit.* "I was writing the article." |
| Indefinite amount | *Söö kook ära!* "Eat up the cake!" | *Söö kooki!* "Have some cake!" |

## Present verb, future meaning (§ 221)

| Total | Partial |
|---|---|
| *Ma parandan ratta.* "I will fix the bike." | *Ma parandan ratast.* "I am fixing the bike." |

## Words that signal completion (§ 222)

Particles such as *ära, läbi, üles, maha, valmis* usually bring a total object.

| Partial | Total |
|---|---|
| *Ta sõi suppi.* "He ate some soup." | *Ta sõi supi ära.* "He ate up the soup." |
| *Õppisin luuletust.* "I was learning the poem." | *Õppisin luuletuse pähe.* "I learned the poem by heart." |

## Verbs that normally take the partitive (§ 223–224)

| Group | Sample verbs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Feelings | *armastama, kartma, vihkama, imetlema* | *Ma vihkan talve.* "I hate winter." |
| Senses | *kuulama, nägema, vaatama, tundma* | *Ta kuulab raadiot.* "She is listening to the radio." |
| Open-ended actions | *ootama, otsima, aitama, segama, õppima, õpetama, tänama, vajama* | *Ta otsib võtit.* "He is looking for the key." |

Some of these take a total object when a particle shows a result:

| Partial | Total |
|---|---|
| *Ma otsisin võtit.* "I was looking for the key." | *Ma otsisin võtme üles.* "I found the key." |
| *Ma tunnen naabrit hästi.* "I know the neighbor well." | *Ma tundsin naabri ära.* "I recognized the neighbor." |

## Always partitive phrases (§ 225)

Some verb + noun phrases keep the partitive even when the action is complete.

| Phrase | English |
|---|---|
| *aega veetma* | to spend time |
| *kätt andma* | to shake hands |
| *pead noogutama* | to nod |
| *vihma sadama* | to rain |

*Mängima* takes the partitive for instruments and games: *Ta mängib kitarri.* "She plays guitar." *Mängisime pool tundi lauatennist.* "We played table tennis for half an hour."

## Pronouns as objects (§ 226–227)

| Pronoun | Total, command | Total, statement | Partial |
|---|---|---|---|
| *tema / ta* | *tema / ta* | *tema / ta* (genitive) | *teda* |
| *nemad / nad* | *nemad / nad* | *nemad / nad* | *neid* |

*Kutsu ta sisse!* "Invite him in!" *Ma ei kutsunud teda.* "I didn't invite him."

First- and second-person pronouns are generally partitive, whether the object is total or partial: *Kutsu mind!* "Invite me!" *Ta kutsus meid külla.* "She invited us over."

In statements, *mina* and *sina* can also be genitive (*minu/mu*, *sinu/su*). *Meid* and *teid* stay partitive.

## Exercises

**A. Put the word in the correct case.**

1. *Ma ostsin ___ (pilet).*
2. *Ma ei ostnud ___ (pilet).*
3. *Osta ___ (ajaleht)!*
4. *Ära osta ___ (ajaleht)!*
5. *Ta otsib ___ (võti).*
6. *Ta leidis ___ (võti).*
7. *Ma lugesin ___ (artikkel) läbi.*
8. *Me ootame ___ (buss).*

**B. Use the pronoun as an object.**

1. *Kutsu ___ (tema) sisse!*
2. *Ma ei näinud ___ (tema).*
3. *Ta kutsus ___ (meie) külla.*
4. *Ma kutsusin ___ (nemad) külla.*
5. *Ära sega ___ (mina)!*

**C. Translate into Estonian.**

1. I will fix the bike.
2. I am fixing the bike.
3. We did not see the film.
4. Eat up the cake!
5. She plays guitar.
