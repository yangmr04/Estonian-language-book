# Lesson 32: Declining Pronouns; *oma* and *end*

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 225 · § 267–281 · [← Previous](lesson-31.md) · [Next →](lesson-33.md)

## Key ideas

- Pronouns use the normal case endings, but many have a short alternative form.
- Relative *kes* and *mis* take the case their role in the clause requires.
- *Oma* points back to the subject, whatever its person.
- *End* or *ennast* is the object form of "myself", "yourself", and so on.

## Personal pronouns (§ 267–268)

| Case | mina | sina | tema | meie | teie | nemad |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gen. | *minu, mu* | *sinu, su* | *tema, ta* | *meie* | *teie* | *nende* |
| Part. | *mind* | *sind* | *teda* | *meid* | *teid* | *neid* |
| Ill. | *minusse, musse* | *sinusse, susse* | *temasse, tasse* | *meisse* | *teisse* | *nendesse, neisse* |
| Iness. | *minus, mus* | *sinus, sus* | *temas, tas* | *meis* | *teis* | *nendes, neis* |
| Elat. | *minust, must* | *sinust, sust* | *temast, tast* | *meist* | *teist* | *nendest, neist* |
| All. | *minule, mulle* | *sinule, sulle* | *temale, talle* | *meile* | *teile* | *nendele, neile* |
| Adess. | *minul, mul* | *sinul, sul* | *temal, tal* | *meil* | *teil* | *nendel, neil* |
| Abl. | *minult, mult* | *sinult, sult* | *temalt, talt* | *meilt* | *teilt* | *nendelt, neilt* |

The other four endings, *-ni*, *-na*, *-ga* and *-ta*, plus translative *-ks*, attach to the long genitive: *minuga*, *sinuta*, *temani*, *meieks*, *nendena*.

## *See*, *too* and *ise* (§ 269)

| Case | see | need | ise (sg.) | ise (pl.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gen. | *selle* | *nende* | *enese, enda* | *eneste, endi* |
| Part. | *seda* | *neid* | *ennast, end* | *endid* |
| Ill. | *sellesse, sesse* | *nendesse, neisse* | *enesesse, endasse* | *enestesse, endisse* |
| Iness. | *selles, ses* | *nendes, neis* | *eneses, endas* | *enestes, endis* |
| Elat. | *sellest, sest* | *nendest, neist* | *enesest, endast* | *enestest, endist* |
| All. | *sellele* | *nendele, neile* | *enesele, endale* | *enestele, endile* |
| Adess. | *sellel, sel* | *nendel, neil* | *enesel, endal* | *enestel, endil* |

- *Too, tolle, toda* means "that one over there". In the plural, *need* normally replaces *nood*.
- *Iseenese*, *iseennast* and similar forms add emphasis.

## Question and relative words (§ 270)

*Kes* and *mis* work as question words and as "who/which/that" in a relative clause. Choose the case from the job inside the clause.

| Example | English | Case and role |
|---|---|---|
| *Naine, keda sa nägid, on minu naaber.* | The woman you saw is my neighbor. | partitive, object |
| *Film, millest ta rääkis, oli igav.* | The film he talked about was boring. | elative, "about" |
| *Mul on naaber, kelle koer haugub öösiti.* | I have a neighbor whose dog barks at night. | genitive, owner |
| *Kes tahab, see võib minna.* | Whoever wants to may go. | nominative, subject |

The adessive has short forms: *kel* (*kellel*) and *mil* (*millel*). The plural forms *kellede* and *millede* are used only to avoid confusion.

## Indefinite pronouns (§ 271–272)

Add *-gi* or *-ki* to the case form of the base word.

| Word | Meaning | Forms |
|---|---|---|
| *keegi* | someone | *kellegi, kedagi, kellelgi, kellestki* |
| *miski* | something | *millegi, midagi, millelgi, millestki* |
| *ükski* | any, a single | *ühegi, ühtegi, ühelgi* |
| *kumbki* | either of two | *kummagi, kumbagi, kummalgi* |

With a negative verb, these words take on the meaning "no one", "nothing", "none":

- *Keegi ei tulnud.* "No one came."
- *Ma ei kuulnud midagi.* "I didn't hear anything."
- *Ükski buss ei sõida.* "No bus is running."

*Kumb* asks "which of the two": *Kumba sa võtad, teed või kohvi?* "Which will you have, tea or coffee?"

## Special cases (§ 273–277)

| Word | Behavior | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *iga, igaüks, kumb, kumbki, ükski* | singular only | *iga päev* |
| *teineteise, üksteise* | "each other", no nominative | *Me aitame üksteist.* |
| *kõik* | same nominative for sg. and pl.; sg. *kõige, kõike*; pl. *kõigi* or *kõikide, kõiki* | *Kõigil on kiire.* |
| *mõlemad* | plural nominative, but usually singular with a noun in other cases | *mõlemad õed*, *mõlemale õele* |
| *kogu* | never changes | *kogu perega* |

*Mõlemaid* appears when no noun follows: *Ma tean mõlemaid.* "I know both."

Other pronouns decline regularly from the genitive:

| Nominative | Genitive | Part. sg. | Part. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| *milline* | *millise* | *millist* | *milliseid* |
| *mingi* | *mingi* | *mingit* | *mingeid* |
| *sama* | *sama* | *sama* | *samu, samasid* |
| *mitmes* | *mitmenda* | *mitmendat* | *mitmendaid* |

## *Oma* (§ 278–280)

Use *oma* when the owner is the subject of the same sentence. It replaces *minu*, *sinu*, *tema*, and the rest.

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Ta pesi oma autot.* | She washed her own car. |
| *Ta pesi tema autot.* | She washed someone else's car. |

- Before a noun, *oma* normally stays the same: *Helistan oma emale.* "I'm phoning my mother."
- Decline it for emphasis: *Ta magab omas voodis.* "She is sleeping in her own bed."
- Use it, declined, when the noun is left out: *Sinu kohver on must, minu oma on hall.* "Your suitcase is black, mine is gray."
- Use it to finish a statement of ownership: *See mantel on Mari oma.* In the plural: *Need võtmed on naabri omad.*

## *End* or *ennast* (§ 281)

These partitive forms work for every person:

| Example | English |
|---|---|
| *Ma vaatan end peeglist.* | I look at myself in the mirror. |
| *Ta lõikas end.* | He cut himself. |
| *Me tutvustasime end.* | We introduced ourselves. |

Other cases follow the *ise* table: *Ta räägib endast.* "He talks about himself."

Nominative *ise* adds a different meaning, "personally": *Ma tegin selle ise.* "I did it myself."
