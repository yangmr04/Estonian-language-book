# Lesson 2: No Articles, Word Order and Questions

[Index](../README.md) · Book: p. 20 · § 8–13 · [← Previous](lesson-01.md) · [Next →](lesson-03.md)

## Key ideas

- Estonian has no words for "a" or "the".
- A plain statement usually runs subject, verb, object.
- Question words and *kas* go first, and the rest of the sentence stays in statement order.
- A "yes" answer can simply repeat the verb, often with *küll*.

## No articles (§ 8)

*Koer magab.* can mean "The dog is sleeping" or "A dog is sleeping". Context decides.

The number *üks* "one" occasionally works like "a (certain)": *Üks naine ootab õues.* "A woman is waiting outside." This is mostly a spoken habit.

## Word order in statements (§ 9)

| Rule | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Subject, verb, object is the default | *Õpetaja joob kohvi.* | The teacher drinks coffee. |
| An adjective comes before its noun | *soe suvi* | a warm summer |
| Time comes before place | *Ema on täna tööl.* | Mother is at work today. |
| An opening adverb or object can pull the verb ahead of the subject | *Täna on ema tööl.* | Today Mother is at work. |

The time-before-place order is the reverse of the usual English pattern. Beyond these tendencies, word order is flexible.

## Question words (§ 10)

Put the question word first and keep statement order. There is no helper like English "do".

| Word | Meaning | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *kes* | who | *Kes laulab?* | Who is singing? |
| *mis* | what | *Mis sa sööd?* | What are you eating? |
| *kus* | where | *Kus te töötate?* | Where do you work? |
| *millal* | when | *Millal rong tuleb?* | When is the train coming? |
| *miks* | why | *Miks koer haugub?* | Why is the dog barking? |
| *kuidas* | how | *Kuidas ta sõidab?* | How is she travelling? |

## Yes/no questions (§ 11–12)

| Pattern | Example | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| *Kas* + statement order | *Kas ema töötab?* | The standard form |
| Verb first, no *kas* | *Töötad sa täna?* | Speech, sometimes writing |
| Statement order, question intonation | *Sa töötad täna?* | Speech |

For the intonation pattern, the voice rises around the verb in the middle of the sentence and falls back at the end. This differs from the English rise on the last word.

## Answering "yes" with the verb (§ 13)

Repeat the verb in the form that fits the person answering. *Küll* adds "indeed, certainly".

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Kas sa töötad täna?* | *Töötan küll.* "Yes, I certainly do." |
| *Kas nad laulavad?* | *Laulavad küll.* "Yes, they do." |

## Useful phrases

| Estonian | English |
|---|---|
| Mis su nimi on? | What is your name? |
| Kust sa pärit oled? | Where are you from? |
| Kas sa räägid inglise keelt? | Do you speak English? |
| Mida see tähendab? | What does this mean? |
| Kus on tualett? | Where is the toilet? |
| Vabandust! | Excuse me! / Sorry! |

## Exercises

**A. Make each statement a question with *kas*.**

1. Koer magab.
2. Sa töötad täna.
3. Nad elavad Pärnus.
4. Õpetaja sööb.

**B. Fill in *kes*, *mis*, *kus* or *millal*.**

1. ___ sa elad? — Tartus.
2. ___ sa jood? — Kohvi.
3. ___ laulab? — Õde laulab.
4. ___ rong tuleb? — Täna õhtul.

**C. Translate into Estonian.**

1. Today the dog is at home. (Start with "today".)
2. Mother is travelling to Tartu tomorrow. (*Tartusse* "to Tartu")
3. Are you coming? (one friend; give two ways)
4. Yes, I certainly am coming. (answer with the verb)
